# ANA KLAUDYA

## Prologue · Following a Card

Following a card,  
I found a place.  
When I stopped there,  
it began to feel like home:  
still without a key,  
but I was already imagining  
my footsteps on returning.

Following an angel,  
I came to you.

When I was yang,  
I searched for yin.

Now that I am yin,  
looking at you is enough.

## A Brief Elegy for Ana Klaudya

There was in her face  
a quiet beauty,  
the kind that never asks  
to be noticed.

The depth of her eyes,  
the line of her mouth,  
her hair arranged  
with a care  
that accentuates  
without correcting.

And then she spoke.  
Brazil remained intact  
in the way she spoke:  
the warm weight of the vowels,  
the lingering syllable,  
a music  
that refused  
to disguise itself.

Then I understood  
that the light of that face  
was still missing  
her voice.

Then came  
the embrace.  
I do not know how long it lasted  
or who moved closer first.  
I remember only  
the warmth.

And there remained as well  
that private injustice  
memory sometimes commits:  
from an entire afternoon  
it keeps intact  
a face,  
and from an entire embrace,

the  
warmth.

## In Your Eyes

Before you smile,  
your eyes  
have already stopped me.

Under your gaze,  
the face I came in with  
falls out of order.

Your eyelids half-close.

I do not know how much of me  
you have already seen.  
And I do not lower my eyes.

---

## On Your Lips

I was going to tell you something.

Your smile begins at the corners of your mouth:  
a slight movement,  
and I am already looking at you  
beyond all prudence.

The gesture spreads across your face.  
I smile too.  
You still have not said a word.

You smile again  
and I remain on your lips,  
never reaching the words.

---

## Your Hair

Black, with that sheen of a mane  
that flashes when you turn your head:  
light glides across it  
without lightening it.

It falls across your shoulder  
and takes its time becoming still.  
You return to your words;  
but one strand prolongs  
the turning of your face.

You brush it aside with your fingers.  
The gesture bares your neck  
and leaves between the strands  
a crack of light.

You keep talking.  
The strand falls back into place.  
I take  
a little longer.

---

## Your Skin

Your skin has that bronze tone.  
In the bend of your elbow  
the colour deepens.

Your hand accompanies your voice.  
You move your fingers  
and the light passes  
from one knuckle to another.

I would like to know that smoothness  
with more than my eyes:  
to rest my hand gently,  
leave it there  
and feel your skin  
warming mine.

---

## Epilogue · Over a Low Flame

I was going to write you a poem.  
You handed me an onion.

—Start with this.

Two minutes later  
you had already made me cry  
without breaking my heart.

You hum something in Portuguese  
while the oil heats up.

I keep the rhythm with my foot.

The spoon  
goes still in my hand.

—Stir, poet.

I had forgotten the stew.  
I was looking for a word  
for the way you smile.

You hold out the spoon.

—Taste it. What does it need?

I keep looking at you.

—The stew, Paco.

—Salt.

You set out two plates.  
I carry the stew to the table.  
You move the papers aside  
to make room for it.

The poem is still unfinished.

You ask me for the bread.  
I give you my hand.

—The bread, poet.

I reach for it with the other.
