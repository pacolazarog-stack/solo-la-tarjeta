## Logline

A gaze, a smile, a voice and the memory of an embrace unfold through poetry, image and sound until a woman ceases to be an image and becomes a presence.

## Synopsis

*ANA KLAUDYA* is a poetry film about the gradual act of seeing another person. A sequence of poems moves from encounter to contemplation: eyes, smile, lips, hair, skin, voice and the remembered warmth of an embrace. Rather than illustrating the words, the film allows image, music, silence and spoken poetry to alter one another. A face slowly gains definition; visual transitions emerge and disappear; the voice enters only after the image has had time to exist on its own. The work moves between desire, restraint, tenderness and the uncertainty of how much of oneself has already been seen by the other. Its final movement returns the lyric experience to an intimate, everyday register, leaving behind not a portrait but the trace of a presence.

## Artist statement

*ANA KLAUDYA* began as a sequence of poems, but the work only became complete when the poems stopped being the sole centre of attention. The image needed time; the voice needed to enter without dominating it; music needed to withdraw rather than underline emotion.

The film is built around progressive revelation. Eyes become clearer without pulsing. A face appears through slow transitions. A smile is not treated as an illustration of a verse but as an event with its own duration. The final question remains unresolved.

The purpose is not to describe a woman exhaustively. It is to record the changing distance between looking and being looked at, between imagining someone and discovering that the other person is also looking back.

## Duration status · corrected 2026-10-07

The current GitHub work is an **interactive audiovisual experience**, not a fixed-length linear film. Its full duration therefore depends on user scrolling, navigation and optional voice playback.

A first code audit shows that the **scripted timed material alone, excluding the scroll-driven Elegy and excluding most navigation time, already amounts to approximately 4 min 55 s**. Once the Elegy, transitions, fades and user interaction are included, the complete experience is substantially longer than 5–6 minutes.

The previously prepared **05:58 Cadence** and **06:48 Midwest** subtitle files are therefore retained only as **hypothetical abbreviated timing studies**. They are **not the canonical duration of ANA KLAUDYA** and must not be used as evidence of running time.

Before festival submission, a separate linear festival master must be defined and timed from picture lock. The Spanish voice must not be mechanically accelerated.
